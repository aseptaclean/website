# 01 — Execution Quality and Anti-Generic Guardrails

**Purpose:** Make the Aseptaclean website feel custom, strategically authored, technically complete, and worth a professional agency investment of approximately **$20,000**—without pretending that price alone proves quality.

**Audience:** Codex and every human reviewing Codex output.

**Applies to:** Every page, component, content block, interaction, responsive state, image, form, and release candidate.

**Authority:** This file is an execution-quality and anti-generic layer. It does not replace the existing strategy, claims, voice, design, SEO, accessibility, or technical specifications.

# 1. The hard truth

A **$20,000 website is not defined by expensive-looking effects**. Price is not a visual style. A site can use large type, dark sections, gradients, scroll animation, and polished mockups and still be strategically weak, generic, inaccessible, or technically careless.

A $20,000-caliber marketing site earns that standard through the combined quality of:

- business and audience understanding
- information architecture
- conversion architecture
- brand-specific art direction
- original page composition
- precise copy
- custom responsive behavior
- complete interaction states
- technical performance
- accessibility
- quality assurance
- maintainability
- honest proof and claims

The user should feel that every visible decision was made **for this company, this buyer, this service, and this moment**.

The site fails this standard when it feels like a polished template with Aseptaclean content poured into it.

---

# 2. Do not claim “50+ years of web experience”

The World Wide Web was invented in 1989. No individual can truthfully have 50 years of web-design experience.

Do not instruct Codex to impersonate a person with an impossible biography. Do not publish or imply that a real designer with 50 years of web experience built the site.

Use this standard instead:

> Apply the judgment, discipline, and review depth expected from a senior multidisciplinary agency team: brand strategist, UX researcher, information architect, conversion copywriter, visual designer, interaction designer, frontend engineer, accessibility specialist, performance engineer, SEO strategist, and QA lead.

These are **review lenses**, not fabricated staff members. Codex must perform a separate pass through each lens and report what changed.

# 3. Research-backed premise

Current UX research on AI-generated interfaces supports 4 conclusions that govern this file:

1. **AI can follow instructions but does not reliably weigh design tradeoffs like an experienced designer.** General direction is not enough.
2. **Vague prompts produce average, inconsistent, and generic interfaces.** Detailed, curated context improves output.
3. **AI-generated interfaces tend to perform better on pragmatic usability than originality and innovation.** Functional does not mean distinctive.
4. **AI explanations cannot be trusted as proof that the requirements were implemented.** The final interface and code must be inspected directly.

Therefore:

- Codex may generate and implement.
- Codex may not self-certify quality with statements such as “premium,” “bespoke,” “accessible,” or “conversion optimized.”
- Every important quality claim must be demonstrated through screenshots, code, test output, or a named artifact.

# 4. Definition of a $20K-caliber Aseptaclean website

The site must be all of the following at the same time.

## 4.1 Strategically specific

A visitor should understand within the first viewport:

- what situation Aseptaclean handles
- who it is for
- why ordinary hauling or cleaning is not enough
- what control the buyer retains
- what action to take next

The page must reflect the buyer's actual decision problem, not merely describe services.

## 4.2 Visually authored

The site must have a discernible visual point of view that could be identified without seeing the logo.

For Aseptaclean, that point of view is **premium operational editorial**:

- calm, not soft
- structured, not bureaucratic
- editorial, not magazine-themed
- premium, not luxurious
- restrained, not empty
- operational, not industrial
- human, not sentimental
- document-led, not document-themed

## 4.3 Easy to use under stress

Most visitors will not explore the site like designers. They may be on a phone, under time pressure, embarrassed, grieving, responsible to other stakeholders, or unsure what the property contains.

The experience must remain clear when the visitor:

- reads only headings
- arrives on a service page instead of the homepage
- uses one hand on a phone
- enlarges text
- navigates by keyboard
- disables JavaScript
- has a slow connection
- is interrupted halfway through a form

## 4.4 Complete at the edges

Premium sites are distinguished by details that budget sites leave unfinished:

- hover states
- focus states
- active states
- visited links where appropriate
- form errors
- success states
- empty states
- long names and long headings
- missing images
- 320px screens
- landscape phones
- browser zoom
- reduced motion
- broken or slow third-party scripts
- 404 and thank-you pages
- footer closure
- social preview images
- print behavior for document-like pages when relevant

## 4.5 Technically quiet

The technology must disappear behind the experience.

No visible layout shift. No delayed font jump. No sticky element covering content. No animation that causes a visitor to wait. No interaction that exists because the developer wanted to demonstrate skill.

# 5. The anti-AI visual blacklist

The following patterns are not automatically forbidden in all design, but they are **forbidden as defaults** in this project because AI website generators overuse them and because they weaken Aseptaclean's distinctiveness.

Codex must not introduce any of these without a page-specific rationale and explicit approval.

## 5.1 Generic hero formulas

- centered eyebrow + giant headline + vague paragraph + 2 equal buttons
- split hero with copy left and random rounded stock image right on every page
- floating dashboard card or fake UI screenshot with no product reason
- decorative gradient orb behind the hero
- 3 badges above or below the headline
- fake star rating, client-logo strip, or stat bar
- full-height hero that pushes the first useful proof below the fold

## 5.2 Generic component furniture

- endless 3-column card grids
- every section inside a rounded rectangle
- every label rendered as a pill
- icon above heading above paragraph repeated 6–12 times
- arbitrary Bento grids
- glassmorphism
- frosted panels
- gradient borders
- glowing buttons
- oversized quote marks
- decorative browser-window mockups
- floating cards that imply software rather than field operations
- generic checkmark lists used as the primary visual device on every page

## 5.3 Generic page rhythm

- hero → logo strip → 3 cards → alternating split → testimonials → FAQ → CTA on every page
- identical hero height across all routes
- identical section spacing across the entire page
- every section centered
- every section left aligned
- alternating image-left/image-right without a decision reason
- repeated dark-light-dark-light bands used mechanically
- identical CTA block after every 2 sections
- the same maximum width applied to headlines, prose, tables, forms, and artifacts

## 5.4 Generic AI art direction

- blue-purple gradients
- vague geometric blobs
- abstract 3D chrome shapes
- shiny glass spheres
- generic “technology” lines and particles
- cinematic stock photographs unrelated to the service
- AI-generated people, properties, documents, crews, or equipment
- obviously staged hands holding a blank tablet
- smiling teams in branded uniforms that do not exist
- fake before-and-after scenes

## 5.5 Generic AI copy

- “At Aseptaclean, we…”
- “We understand that…”
- “Whether you are…”
- “From X to Y, we…”
- “Our comprehensive solutions…”
- “Navigate the complexities…”
- “Seamless,” “robust,” “cutting-edge,” or “best-in-class”
- “It is not just X. It is Y.”
- 3 parallel benefits with suspiciously equal sentence length
- empty transition paragraphs
- rhetorical questions used as section headings
- paragraphs written to fill a layout socket
- the same CTA repeated verbatim throughout the page

## 5.6 Generic motion

- every element fading upward on scroll
- staggered card reveals on every grid
- parallax
- scroll-jacking
- cursor effects
- animated gradient backgrounds
- continuous marquees
- number counters without real verified numbers
- button hover effects that move more than 2–3px
- animation that hides content until JavaScript runs

# 6. Required signs of human authorship

Every page must contain evidence of real judgment. These are not decorative requirements. They are signals that the page was composed for its specific job.

## 6.1 A page thesis

Before building, state in one sentence:

> This page helps [specific visitor] decide [specific decision] by proving [specific mechanism].

If the sentence is vague, the page is not ready to design.

## 6.2 A composition fingerprint

Every page plan must declare:

- hero type and why it fits
- first proof after the hero
- primary signature moment
- densest section
- quietest section
- where the main visual reset occurs
- primary CTA
- secondary CTA
- mobile section order
- desktop section order
- one layout behavior not reused from the previous 2 pages

No 2 service pages may share the same full sequence.

## 6.3 A dominant signature moment

Every major page gets **one** memorable, brand-owned moment, not 5 competing tricks.

Permitted Aseptaclean signature moments include:

- full-scale Defined Scope Document artifact
- Found Property Protocol artifact
- scope-control matrix
- fixed-scope change-authorization demonstration
- annotated completion-record structure
- decision pathway built around authority and approval
- location-specific logistics image and explanation
- a typography-led guarantee statement

The signature must help explain or prove something. It may not exist only to look impressive.

## 6.4 Specific irregularity

Premium design often contains controlled asymmetry and variation. AI defaults toward overly even compositions.

Each page must include at least 2 deliberate irregularities, such as:

- a 7/5 editorial split instead of 6/6
- a section with a narrow text column beside a wider document artifact
- one compact band between 2 spacious sections
- one off-grid rule or annotation tied to the document system
- a card group with one earned wide item rather than equal tiles
- a mobile reordering decision based on urgency

Irregularity must improve hierarchy. Random imbalance fails.

## 6.5 Real content shapes the layout

Codex must not design with placeholder lengths and pour final copy in later.

Headings, paragraphs, tables, exclusions, pricing drivers, forms, and document labels must be substantially final before visual polish. The layout must adapt to the real content, not force the content to fit a preselected template.

# 7. Art-direction guardrails

## 7.1 One image has one job

Every image must do at least one of these:

1. establish a real property or local context
2. explain an operational object or process
3. create a deliberate visual pause
4. provide authentic proof
5. establish founder accountability

“Make the section less empty” is not a valid job.

## 7.2 Build the image manifest before implementation

For every image, record:

- filename
- source
- license
- date acquired
- photographer or creator where required
- page and section
- purpose
- whether authentic, editorial, or illustrative
- whether it could be mistaken for an Aseptaclean project
- alt-text intent
- mobile crop
- desktop crop
- fallback treatment

No image enters production without the manifest.

## 7.3 No deceptive polish

Do not use an image because it makes the company look larger, older, busier, more credentialed, or more experienced than it is.

Premium does not mean inflated.

## 7.4 Cohesive image treatment

All approved images must share an intentional treatment:

- compatible color temperature
- controlled saturation
- consistent contrast
- related crop logic
- no mixed collection of warm lifestyle stock, cold industrial stock, and AI art

A single bad stock image can make the entire site feel templated.

## 7.5 Responsive art direction

Do not rely on `object-fit: cover` as the entire mobile strategy.

For every important image:

- define the mobile focal point
- confirm no subject is awkwardly cropped
- confirm text and image order makes sense
- use a separate crop or source when the desktop composition fails on mobile
- specify intrinsic dimensions to prevent layout shift

# 8. Typography and editorial craft

Typography must perform hierarchy, pacing, and tone. It may not simply decorate headings.

## 8.1 Required hierarchy

Every page must visibly distinguish:

- page title
- major section heading
- subsection heading
- lead paragraph
- body paragraph
- label or eyebrow
- annotation or caption
- table label
- CTA text

No 3 consecutive text elements may rely on size alone for hierarchy.

Use weight, line height, width, spacing, contrast, and placement deliberately.

## 8.2 Line-breaking review

Every H1 and H2 must be reviewed at:

- 320px
- 390px
- 768px
- 1024px
- 1280px
- 1440px

Reject:

- single-word widows
- awkward orphaned prepositions where avoidable
- a second line containing only 1–2 short words
- headings that create accidental shapes
- highlighted words wrapping separately from their phrase

Manual line breaks are permitted only when they remain sound across responsive ranges or are controlled by breakpoint-specific markup that preserves semantics.

## 8.3 Editorial measure

- Long prose should remain near the approved readable measure.
- Captions, metadata, and annotations use narrower measures.
- Headlines may use wider measures only when the line count and hierarchy remain controlled.
- Forms, tables, and artifacts use widths based on task, not the body-copy width.

## 8.4 Punctuation and rhythm

Copy must pass `03-VOICE.md` and then receive a second visual read in the browser.

A sentence that is good in a document may produce an ugly, dense, or repetitive layout. Edit the copy and composition together.

# 9. Layout and spacing craft

## 9.1 No mechanical spacing

Tokens are a foundation, not permission to use identical spacing everywhere.

Each page plan must map sections to:

- tight
- standard
- spacious
- custom justified exception

Three consecutive sections with the same surface, spacing, alignment, and density fail.

## 9.2 One focal point per section

At first glance, a section should have one dominant element:

- headline
- artifact
- guarantee
- image
- matrix
- form
- number

Two equal visual anchors create indecision. Demote one.

## 9.3 Cards must be earned

Use a card only when its boundary communicates a meaningful unit, choice, state, comparison, or object.

Do not turn normal prose into cards merely to create visual variety.

Before adding a card, Codex must answer:

> What becomes clearer because this content has a boundary?

If the answer is “nothing,” remove the card.

## 9.4 Surface changes must mean something

A dark section should indicate a shift in importance, mechanism, cost honesty, guarantee, or conversion—not simply relieve visual boredom.

A caution surface is for exclusions or stop conditions.

A signal surface is for guarantees or confirmed commitments.

Paper-white is reserved for document artifacts and the brightest evidence moments.

## 9.5 Footer closure

The footer must feel like a deliberate ending, not an SEO link dump.

It must close the visual rhythm, preserve navigation utility, show current business facts, include required disclaimers, and offer a final clear path without repeating the entire homepage.

# 10. Interaction and motion craft

## 10.1 Interaction inventory

Every interactive element must define:

- default
- hover
- focus-visible
- active or pressed
- disabled when applicable
- loading when applicable
- success or selected when applicable
- error when applicable

A component is incomplete until all relevant states exist.

## 10.2 Motion hierarchy

Motion is assigned by importance:

1. **Functional:** menu, accordion, form response, sticky-bar entrance
2. **Feedback:** hover, press, focus transition
3. **Narrative:** rare section reveal or artifact emphasis
4. **Decorative:** generally prohibited

Functional motion wins the budget. Decorative motion is removed first.

## 10.3 Motion limits

- CSS-first
- no animation library without approval
- default durations approximately 150–250ms
- transforms remain subtle
- opacity-only content reveals must not leave information inaccessible
- no motion required to understand sequence or status
- `prefers-reduced-motion` must disable nonessential motion

## 10.4 No reveal dependency

All content must exist and remain readable before motion initializes. An IntersectionObserver may enhance presence; it may not gate access.

# 11. Mobile must be art-directed independently

A premium mobile site is not the desktop site stacked vertically.

## 11.1 Mobile plan required before code

For each page, specify:

- what appears in the first viewport
- whether the primary CTA is visible without scrolling
- what content moves earlier because mobile users need it sooner
- what image is removed, recropped, or reduced
- what table becomes labeled blocks
- what decorative element disappears
- where sticky actions appear and disappear
- what content is compressed
- what remains spacious despite the smaller screen

## 11.2 Thumb and interruption test

At 390px, a visitor using one hand must be able to:

- call
- text a photo where appropriate
- open and close navigation
- understand the current page
- reach the form
- correct an error
- return to the page after interruption

without accidental taps or hidden controls.

## 11.3 Mobile density

Do not solve mobile by shrinking type below the locked minimum.

Change:

- layout
- order
- number of visible options
- media size
- spacing
- content length where the brief permits

before reducing text size.

# 12. Performance and technical bar

The existing Aseptaclean performance targets are stricter than Google's general “good” thresholds and remain the project target.

## Required project targets

- LCP under **2.0 seconds** in the defined mobile test
- INP under **200ms**
- CLS under **0.05**
- total shipped JavaScript under the active build budget
- total page weight under the active build budget
- Lighthouse mobile performance **95+**
- accessibility **100**
- best practices **100**
- SEO **100**

Passing Lighthouse once on a developer machine is not proof of field performance. Record both lab results and field data after launch when available.

## 12.1 Code-quality guardrails

- semantic HTML before ARIA
- no JavaScript for behavior native HTML can provide
- no duplicated component logic without a documented reason
- no inline event handlers
- no dead CSS
- no unscoped global selector that can unexpectedly affect WordPress or another route
- no layout built from magic pixel offsets
- no `!important` except a documented integration or accessibility override
- no hidden horizontal overflow used to conceal a broken layout
- no accessibility overlay as a substitute for accessible code
- no third-party script without owner, purpose, privacy review, and performance cost

## 12.2 Font quality

- load only needed families and weights
- preload only what materially improves the first viewport
- provide fallbacks with compatible metrics where supported
- prevent FOIT and large layout shift
- do not add a new display font to make one page feel different

## 12.3 Image quality

- AVIF/WebP where supported by the active stack
- explicit dimensions
- responsive source sizes
- correct priority for the LCP asset
- lazy-load below the fold
- no oversized original sent to a small viewport
- no blurry upscaling

# 13. Accessibility is part of premium execution

The site must meet WCAG 2.2 AA. The project should exceed the minimum where practical.

## 13.1 Target sizes

- interactive targets: **48px preferred**
- never below the active project minimum
- adequate spacing between adjacent controls

## 13.2 Focus

- visible focus state on every interactive element
- focus must not be obscured by sticky headers, drawers, or bottom bars
- focus order follows reading order
- modal or mobile-menu focus is managed correctly
- Escape closes dismissible overlays

## 13.3 Content

- one H1
- logical heading order
- landmarks
- descriptive labels
- errors connected to their fields
- no information conveyed by color alone
- alt text describes purpose, not keywords
- decorative images use empty alt text

## 13.4 Zoom and reflow

Test at:

- 200% browser zoom
- 400% text or equivalent reflow check where applicable
- 320px CSS width

No essential content or control may disappear.

# 14. Conversion quality without funnel theater

## 14.1 One dominant action

Each page has one primary action. Secondary actions support it but do not compete equally.

## 14.2 CTA language reflects the next step

Use specific actions:

- Request a Property Assessment
- Call and Describe the Property
- Text a Photo
- See What the Assessment Covers
- View the Cost Drivers
- Download the Vendor Documentation Packet

Do not default to “Learn More,” “Get Started,” or “Contact Us.”

## 14.3 No false urgency

No countdowns, limited-time claims, pulsing buttons, fake availability, fake recent inquiries, or manufactured danger.

## 14.4 Forms are part of the design

A form must have:

- visible labels
- logical grouping
- no unnecessary required fields
- plain consent language
- useful errors
- a clear success state
- a working no-JavaScript path where required
- a visible phone alternative if submission fails

# 15. Content and credibility bar

## 15.1 The swap test

Replace “Aseptaclean” with a competitor's name.

If the first 60 words, hero, or core section still works, it fails.

## 15.2 The evidence test

For every strong statement, classify it as:

- verifiable fact
- process commitment
- service description
- conditional outcome
- clearly labeled sample
- unsupported claim

Unsupported claims are removed or surfaced for owner verification.

## 15.3 The specificity test

At least once per major section, the copy must include a concrete operational detail that proves the page knows the situation.

Examples:

- 3 family members disagree about what leaves
- documents and photographs are set aside
- scope changes require a signature
- parking or stairs change logistics
- work stops on an unclassified item

The detail must be relevant, not inserted mechanically.

## 15.4 No synthetic proof

No AI-generated testimonial, client quote, project photograph, statistic, badge, rating, case-study result, partner relationship, or review excerpt.

# 16. The $20K build workflow

Codex must not jump from brief to final page.

## Stage 1 — Strategic framing

Deliver before design:

- page thesis
- primary audience
- search intent
- decision problem
- primary objection
- conversion goal
- required proof
- claim risks
- content inventory

## Stage 2 — Comparative teardown

Select 3–5 relevant references. For each, identify:

- what works
- what is weak
- what is inappropriate for Aseptaclean
- one transferable principle
- one pattern to reject

Do not copy the reference's section sequence.

## Stage 3 — Composition exploration

Produce **2 materially different wireframe directions**, not color variations.

Each direction must differ in:

- hero structure
- information sequence
- signature moment placement
- surface rhythm
- CTA timing
- mobile emphasis

Critique both. Select one and explain the tradeoff.

## Stage 4 — Content-first structure

Place substantially real copy into the selected wireframe. Resolve content length, hierarchy, and missing facts before high-fidelity polish.

## Stage 5 — High-fidelity design

Apply the locked design system. Introduce only page-specific variation justified by the composition fingerprint.

## Stage 6 — Implementation

Build in logical sections. After each major section group, compare the implementation to the approved plan at mobile and desktop sizes.

## Stage 7 — Multi-lens review

Run separate reviews as:

1. brand strategist
2. UX researcher
3. information architect
4. conversion copywriter
5. visual designer
6. interaction designer
7. frontend engineer
8. accessibility specialist
9. performance engineer
10. SEO strategist
11. QA lead

For each lens, report:

- failure found
- evidence
- change made
- remaining limitation

“No issues” is not accepted without evidence.

## Stage 8 — Anti-AI audit

Run the blacklist in §5 and the scoring system in §20.

## Stage 9 — Release verification

Provide screenshots, test results, and a final pass/fail report. Do not describe the page as complete until the evidence exists.

# 18. Anti-AI forensic audit

A page fails when 3 or more of the following are present, even if each one seems acceptable alone:

- generic split hero
- blue gradient or decorative orb
- pills used for most labels
- 3 equal cards directly below hero
- same border radius everywhere
- same shadow everywhere
- same section padding everywhere
- repeated centered section introductions
- alternating image/text splits without purpose
- more than 2 generic icon groups
- equal-weight CTAs
- vague headline
- vague supporting paragraph
- 3-item benefit list with equal copy length
- fake proof socket
- generic stock photograph
- excessive fade-up motion
- footer that looks like a template default
- copy that passes the competitor-name swap test
- no page-specific signature moment
- no documented mobile composition decision

A page also fails immediately for any one of these critical signs:

- fabricated proof
- copied competitor composition
- inaccessible core navigation or form
- obvious broken responsive state
- animation blocking content
- performance budget breach without approved exception
- unverified regulated-service claim

# 19. Design-theater prevention

Codex must not receive credit for explaining a design principle. It receives credit only when the implementation demonstrates it.

For every end-of-session statement such as:

- “clear hierarchy”
- “mobile-first”
- “accessible”
- “premium”
- “custom”
- “performance optimized”
- “conversion focused”

Codex must attach evidence.

Examples:

| Claim | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Clear hierarchy | screenshot with named focal point per section |
| Mobile-first | mobile order plan + 320/390 screenshots |
| Accessible | keyboard test + automated audit + manual focus review |
| Custom | composition fingerprint + anti-AI audit |
| Performance optimized | Lighthouse output + asset and JS totals |
| Conversion focused | page thesis + CTA hierarchy + form path |
| Distinct page | page-sequence comparison against adjacent routes |

A convincing explanation with no implementation evidence is a failure.

# 20. $20K quality score

Score every completed page out of 100.

## 20.1 Strategy and brand specificity — 20 points

- 5: page thesis is precise
- 5: buyer and decision problem are unmistakable
- 5: copy and visuals are Aseptaclean-specific
- 5: page strengthens the written-scope position

## 20.2 Information architecture and conversion — 15 points

- 5: section sequence matches visitor psychology
- 4: primary action is clear
- 3: objections and boundaries appear at the right time
- 3: internal links support the next decision

## 20.3 Visual direction and craft — 20 points

- 5: strong composition fingerprint
- 4: disciplined typography
- 4: controlled spacing and surface rhythm
- 4: one earned signature moment
- 3: imagery or artifacts are intentional and cohesive

## 20.4 UX and accessibility — 15 points

- 4: mobile is independently art-directed
- 3: navigation and interactions are clear
- 3: keyboard and focus behavior pass
- 3: form and error states pass
- 2: zoom, reflow, and target sizes pass

## 20.5 Technical execution and performance — 15 points

- 5: performance budget passes
- 3: no layout shift or responsive breakage
- 3: semantic, maintainable implementation
- 2: images and fonts optimized
- 2: JavaScript minimal and resilient

## 20.6 Content and credibility — 10 points

- 4: voice matches `03-VOICE.md`
- 3: claims pass the guardrails
- 3: no filler, generic, or synthetic proof

## 20.7 QA and completeness — 5 points

- 2: required screenshot set reviewed
- 1: browser/device checks pass
- 1: no unfinished states
- 1: report is evidence-based

## Ship threshold

- **90–100:** $20K-caliber candidate
- **85–89:** strong but not ready; fix visible weaknesses
- **75–84:** professional template quality; does not meet the brief
- **below 75:** rebuild the page plan, not merely the CSS

### Noncompensable failures

A high score cannot offset a critical failure. Any critical failure in §18 blocks release.

- container logic

# 23. Reference standard for “premium”

Do not treat awards galleries as conversion truth. Use them for craft references only.

A premium Aseptaclean page must balance:

- **content** — useful, specific, accurate
- **structure and navigation** — obvious pathways
- **visual design** — appropriate, cohesive, memorable
- **functionality** — works across states and devices
- **interactivity** — enhances rather than obstructs
- **overall experience** — feels more considered than the sum of its sections

Novelty is optional. Coherence is mandatory.

# 24. Final review questions

Before approving a page, answer honestly:

1. Could this layout belong to a SaaS company, law firm, or cleaning company with only the copy changed?
2. Is the most memorable thing on the page owned by Aseptaclean's strategy?
3. Does the page help the visitor decide, or merely keep them scrolling?
4. Did the actual content determine the composition?
5. Is any section present only because websites usually have one?
6. Does mobile have its own hierarchy?
7. Is every image necessary and non-deceptive?
8. Are the exclusions as well designed as the capabilities?
9. Are there visible details a cheap template would normally omit?
10. Can every claim of quality be proven with an artifact or test?
11. Does the page feel calm because it is controlled, or empty because it lacks content?
12. Would a skeptical fiduciary and an overwhelmed family member both find a credible path?
13. Is the page distinct from the previous 2 pages without breaking the brand?
14. Did any AI default survive merely because it looked clean?
15. Would an elite human reviewer know why each major decision was made?

Any evasive answer is a revision request.

# 28. Final principle

> AI may accelerate production. It may not lower the standard of judgment.

The goal is not to disguise that tools were used. The goal is to produce work so specific, coherent, tested, and complete that the tool is irrelevant to the visitor.
