# PART I — GLOBAL LANDING PAGE DESIGN SYSTEM

## 1. OVERARCHING THEME

The service landing pages should feel:

**Calm. Precise. Human. Serious. Clean. Premium. Operational.**

Aseptaclean should not look like:

- a maid company
- a junk hauler
- a restoration franchise
- a biohazard company trying to scare people
- an AI-generated home-services template

It should feel like a serious property-cleanup company that looks at the condition, builds a plan, defines the scope, and then does the work.

The website should communicate:

> **We understand difficult property conditions. We look first. We set the scope. Then we do the work.**

The website should never use fear, hype, fake urgency, or exaggerated promises to make a sale.

Aseptaclean earns trust by showing:

- what the condition is
- what matters
- what can be done
- what cannot be done
- what happens next
- what the customer is paying for

---

## 2. CENTRAL BRAND IDEA

Every landing page should reinforce the same operating philosophy:

## See the problem.
## Set the plan.
## Do the work.
## Check the result.

This is the thread that connects all Aseptaclean services.

Different services have different problems, but the company always approaches them through a clear process.

---

## 3. EACH PAGE IS A SPECIALIST

Do **not** build one generic service-page template and swap one service name for another.

Each landing page should feel like a specialist salesperson who only handles that one problem.

The visual system stays consistent. The **sales argument changes**.

### Rodent Droppings
> The visible droppings may not be the whole affected area.

### Hoarding
> You stay in control of what stays and what leaves.

### Detailed Deep Cleaning
> This is more work and more detail than normal housekeeping.

### Move-In Cleaning
> Clean the home while everything is still open and easy to reach.

### Move-Out Cleaning
> Get the property ready for the next handoff.

### Estate / Property Cleanout
> Protect what matters before the property gets cleared.

### Animal Waste
> The visible waste and the affected material underneath may be different problems.

### Pigeon Droppings
> Heavy dry buildup needs different handling from ordinary dirt.

### Extreme Property Cleanup
> The condition is past normal housekeeping and needs a property plan.

The visitor should feel:

> **These people deal with exactly what I have.**

---

## 4. VISUAL LANGUAGE

The pages should have a strong editorial/restoration feel.

Think:

- large typography
- strong photography
- generous negative space
- restrained colors
- deliberate asymmetry
- clear section changes
- large numbers
- simple process diagrams
- real captions
- subtle rules and borders
- occasional technical/document-style elements

Avoid:

- endless rounded cards
- nine identical service cards
- gradient blobs
- floating icons everywhere
- giant pills
- excessive shadows
- fake dashboards
- random glassmorphism
- decorative 3D objects
- AI illustrations
- over-animated sections
- every section centered
- every section having the same width and layout

The design should feel made by a human with a point of view, not generated from a component library.

---

## 5. DO NOT BUILD STACKED COMPONENT PAGES

Avoid this pattern:

**Hero → 3 cards → 4 cards → 3 cards → FAQ accordion → CTA**

Instead, create visual rhythm.

Example rhythm:

1. Large photo-led hero
2. Editorial problem-recognition section
3. Asymmetric condition section
4. Narrow technical callout
5. Large process sequence
6. Full-width proof photography
7. Split-column qualification section
8. Pricing
9. FAQ
10. Large final CTA

The layout should change based on what the content is trying to do.

---

Every landing page begins with **search confirmation**.

The visitor should instantly know they landed on the right page.

### Eyebrow
Location / service context.

Example:

> San Jose & Santa Clara County

### H1
Exact service.

Example:

> Rodent Droppings Cleanup

Do not sacrifice clarity for a clever headline.

### Supporting Statement

### Primary CTA
Specific to that service.

Do not use generic:

> Contact Us

Examples:

- Send Photos for Review
- Tell Us About the Property
- Request a Deep Clean Estimate
- Check My Move-In Date
- Get My Move-Out Estimate

### Secondary CTA
Phone.

---

## 7. HERO IMAGE RULE

The image must reinforce the service.

Use actual Aseptaclean work whenever possible.

Never use:

- cleaners smiling at the camera
- staged cleaning teams
- stock families
- random houses
- fake hazmat suits
- AI-generated contamination
- hands holding spray bottles

Show:

**the condition**

or

**the work**

or

**the result**

---

## 8. CONDITION RECOGNITION

Very early on the page, show the customer situations you handle.

The purpose is:

> **Yes. This looks like my problem.**

The landing pages should show **specific conditions**, not generic service features.

---

## 9. ONE STRONG EDUCATIONAL IDEA

Every specialty page should teach the visitor one useful thing they probably did not know.

This creates authority without bragging.

Examples:

- Rodent: Do not dry sweep or vacuum untreated droppings.
- Pigeon: Heavy dry buildup should not simply be disturbed into dust.
- Animal Waste: Surface cleaning may not fix urine that has reached absorbent materials.
- Hoarding: The item decision process should be established before clearing begins.
- Move-In: The empty-home window gives access that disappears once furniture and belongings arrive.
- Move-Out: Cleaning and damage are different problems.
- Extreme Cleanup: You cannot clean surfaces you cannot reach; clearing may have to come first.

This should feel like a field note or technical note, not an alarm banner.

---

## 10. EXPLAIN THE PROBLEM BEFORE SELLING THE SOLUTION

The visitor should understand why the obvious/basic solution may not be enough.

Examples:

- Why isn't sweeping enough?
- Why isn't a normal cleaning enough?
- Why can't we just throw everything away?
- Why doesn't deodorizer fix the problem?
- Why shouldn't we start with junk removal?
- Why isn't this simply a longer deep clean?

This creates the need for the service without exaggerated fear.

---

## 11. CLEAR SCOPE

Aseptaclean should become known for unusually clear scopes.

Every page should eventually answer:

## What may be included?

and

## What is not included?

This is a major trust feature and reduces:

> I thought that was included.

---

## 12. PROCESS VISUALIZATION

Use a numbered process.

Do not put every process inside four small cards.

It should feel like a field procedure.

Example:

01 — Show Us the Problem  
02 — We Review the Condition  
03 — We Set the Scope  
04 — We Do the Work  
05 — We Check the Result

Some services will have different steps. Follow the actual job.

---

## 13. PROOF SYSTEM

Because Aseptaclean is still building traditional social proof, the website should use **evidence proof** aggressively.

### Level 1 — Real project photography
Before. During. After.

### Level 2 — Specific captions
Example:

> Heavy rodent droppings along garage shelving.

### Level 3 — Scope description
Example:

> Scope: shelving, perimeter flooring, and accessible hard surfaces.

### Level 4 — Project context
Example:

> Residential garage — Dublin, CA

### Level 5 — Testimonials
Add as they become available.

### Level 6 — Case studies
Eventually:

- condition
- challenge
- scope
- process
- outcome

---

## 14. BEFORE / DURING / AFTER

For Aseptaclean, **during** matters.

Anyone can post Dirty → Clean.

Showing the process proves that there is actually a method behind the work.

Use:

### BEFORE
What we found.

### DURING
What the cleanup looked like.

### AFTER
What the finished area looked like.

For property cleanouts or hoarding:

### START
### SORTED
### CLEARED
### CLEANED

---

## 15. QUALIFY THE VISITOR

Every landing page should contain:

## This May Be a Good Fit If...

and

## You May Not Need Us If...

A premium company should repel bad-fit work.

This builds credibility because the website is not trying to sell everyone.

---

## 16. PRICING

Do not hide pricing where a useful starting price exists.

For detailed cleaning pages:

> **Qualifying 2-bed / 2-bath homes start at $1,350.**

Then explain what changes price.

For specialty cleanup:

Do not invent fake starting prices if the condition varies too much.

Instead explain the price drivers and tell the visitor to start with photos.

---

## 17. OBJECTION HANDLING

Each landing page gets its own FAQ.

Do not copy generic FAQs everywhere.

Questions should come from the actual buying decision.

---

## 18. FINAL CTA

The final CTA should feel like a natural conclusion.

Do not use:

> READY TO EXPERIENCE THE ASEPTACLEAN DIFFERENCE?

Use language matched to the service and customer's state.

---

## 19. FORMS CHANGE BY SERVICE

Do not use the same contact form on every page.

The first questions should help scope **that specific job**.

The page itself should start the sales process.

---

Ask only what helps determine:

- fit
- scope
- urgency
- next step

Primary contact fields:

- Name
- Phone
- City

Then 3–6 service-specific questions.

Photos should be available where they materially help.

## 22. DESIGN HIERARCHY

Each section gets one job.

1. Confirm service.
2. Recognize condition.
3. Teach.
4. Explain why the obvious solution fails.
5. Show method.
6. Define scope.
7. Show proof.
8. Qualify.
9. Explain price.
10. Answer objections.
11. Convert.

---

## 23. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography should feel strong and restrained.

Avoid:

- giant SaaS-style headings everywhere
- all-uppercase paragraphs
- five font weights in one section
- excessive bolding

Use:

- one strong heading family
- one highly readable body family
- clear H1 / H2 / body / label hierarchy
- controlled line length
- carefully controlled wrapping
- short paragraphs

---

## 24. COPY STYLE

The reading level should be very easy, but the thinking should remain sophisticated.

Use:

- short sentences
- common words
- active voice
- concrete examples
- short paragraphs
- useful headings

Avoid childish language.

The goal is:

**easy to understand, not dumb.**

---

## 25. COPY SHOULD HAVE A POINT OF VIEW

Examples:

> A cleared house is not always a clean house.

> Cleaning and damage are not the same thing.

> You cannot clean a floor you cannot reach.

> More scrubbing is not always the answer.

> Cleanup fixes the mess. It does not stop the birds from coming back.

> We do not want to turn a small problem into a large cleanup job.

These lines make the brand feel experienced rather than generated.

---

## 26. DO NOT OVERSELL CREDENTIALS

Credentials should support the argument.

They should not become the argument.

The visitor cares first about:

> Can you solve this problem?

Credentials help answer:

> Can I trust you to do it?

---

## 27. NO FEAR-BASED SALES

Especially on rodent, pigeon, animal waste, and future biohazard pages.

Do not use fear-driven disease claims.

Use legitimate health or safety information only when it helps the customer understand proper handling.

**Authority > fear.**

---

## 28. LOCAL SIGNALS

Use the location naturally.

Example:

> San Jose & Santa Clara County

Real project captions may include real cities when appropriate.

Do not keyword-stuff location names.

---

## 29. MICRO-INTERACTIONS ONLY

Good:

- subtle button response
- image reveal
- slight hover treatment
- restrained number animation
- simple accordion transition
- photo comparison interaction

Bad:

- flying cards
- scroll hijacking
- dramatic parallax
- spinning icons
- constant motion
- animated gradients
- animations that delay readability

---

## 30. PERFORMANCE

Pages should:

- use modern image formats
- serve responsive sizes
- lazy-load below-fold images
- avoid oversized video
- minimize JS
- avoid unnecessary libraries
- avoid heavy animation
- reserve image dimensions to stop layout shift

A premium page that loads slowly is not premium.

---

## 31. COLOR SYSTEM

Use the Aseptaclean palette with restraint.

Large areas should mostly use:

- warm white
- deep navy
- soft neutral backgrounds

Accent colors should guide:

- CTAs
- numbers
- key labels
- small visual signals

Do not make every section a different color.

---

## 32. OVERALL LANDING PAGE RHYTHM

### A. CONFIRM
Exact service. Strong image. Clear CTA.

### B. RECOGNIZE
Show the visitor's exact condition.

### C. EDUCATE
Teach one important thing.

### D. REFRAME
Explain why the obvious/basic solution may not be enough.

### E. DEFINE
Explain what Aseptaclean does and does not do.

### F. PROCESS
Show how the job works.

### G. PROVE
Real project imagery and evidence.

### H. QUALIFY
Who should and should not hire Aseptaclean.

### I. PRICE
Give a starting price or explain price drivers.

### J. REMOVE DOUBT
Service-specific FAQs.

### K. CONVERT
One clear next step.

---

## 33. TARGET CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

The page should make the visitor think:

> **This is exactly the problem I have.**

Then:

> **They understand it better than a normal cleaner.**

Then:

> **I understand what they actually do.**

Then:

> **They are not trying to scare me or sell me work I don't need.**

Then:

> **I know what happens next.**

Then:

> **I should send them photos / request the estimate / tell them about the property.**

The website should not win because it has the most copy.

It should win because **every section answers the next question in the customer's head.**
