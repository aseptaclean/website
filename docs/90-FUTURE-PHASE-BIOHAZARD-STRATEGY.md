# 1. Positioning spine (retained from original draft)

## Core brand idea

> **When routine cleaning is not enough.**

Aseptaclean sells the appropriate level of intervention for the property condition —
not "better cleaning." This is the sentence that lets a $2,000 detail reset, a
$10,000 severe-condition cleanup, and a future high-value biohazard project coexist
under one coherent brand. The brand is the diagnostic authority, not the labor.

## Service spectrum at full activation

Detail Property Reset → Severe-Condition Cleanup → Biohazard Cleanup

## The reframe mechanic (retained — core sales argument)

Do not attack other cleaning companies. Explain that the customer may have been
buying the wrong category of service.

> Routine cleaning is designed to maintain a home. It is not designed to correct
> accumulated condition, neglected detail areas, or biological contamination.

This converts the customer's past disappointment into a rational reason Aseptaclean
costs more, without naming or disparaging anyone.

# 2. Pillar name and vocabulary rules

The third pillar is named **Biohazard Cleanup**. Full stop.

The original draft's "Biohazard & Contamination Cleanup" is rejected as a category
name. Reasons:

- "Contamination" as a category label bleeds into animal/organic pages the moment
  anyone cross-references, violating current vocabulary rules for that scope.
- "Contamination" language activates only with the claims-guardrails amendment
  (Gate 3), and even then only where technically accurate on a specific surface or
  condition — never as a brand-level category word.

| Permitted (post-amendment, where accurate) | Never permitted |
| --- | --- |
| biohazard cleanup | decontamination (as a service name) |
| biological contamination (specific, situational) | sterilization |

| bodily fluid cleanup | "safe" / "sanitized" as an outcome promise |
| trauma scene cleanup | medical, clinical, or health-outcome claims |

# 4. Buyer, mindset, tone (retained with corrections)

## Buyer

Families, homeowners, property managers, landlords, estate representatives,
fiduciaries, businesses, caregivers.

## Buyer mindset

> "I need this handled correctly, discreetly, safely, and quickly."

The buyer does not want marketing. They want answers: Can you handle this? What
happens next? Do I have to touch anything? Will you be discreet? How quickly?

## Tone rules (retained verbatim — these were right)

- Calm. Procedural. Competent. Discreet.
- Short sentences. No dramatic imagery. No fear tactics. No exaggerated claims.
- Never: disgusting, nasty, shame language, fear-based language.
- Use: property condition, cleanup, defined scope, structured process, controlled
  procedures.

# 5. Guarantee layer (NEW — replaces transparency-only trust strategy)

The original draft's trust strategy was transparency-only. Transparency is table
stakes. Named, falsifiable guarantees are the differentiator, consistent with the
mechanic already deployed on the live site's commercial pages. Each guarantee is a
forward commitment Aseptaclean controls — never a past-performance claim.

Candidate named guarantees for the biohazard pillar (final names and terms require
owner approval and claims check at activation):

## The Written Scope Guarantee

No work begins without a written scope. Conditions discovered outside the approved
scope stop work and are discussed before anything additional proceeds. Falsifiable:
the customer either has the document before work starts, or the guarantee failed.

## The Discretion Guarantee (signature move — productize the #1 fear)

The original draft identified discretion as the buyer's leading fear and then left
it as an FAQ answer. That is a whiff. Name it and commit to it:

- Unmarked vehicle at the property.
- No company name stated to neighbors, visitors, or callers at the site.
- No condition details discussed with anyone other than the authorizing client.
- No photographs used publicly, ever, without written consent — default is never.

Every element is falsifiable on the day of service. No competitor in this category
names discretion as a guarantee. This is the pillar's signature commitment.

## The Documented Closeout Guarantee

The project ends with a written closeout record of what was performed against the
approved scope. Extends the live site's documentation-first wedge into the highest-
stakes category, where paper matters most (estates, insurers, fiduciaries).

Rule: guarantees carry forward even after reviews and case studies exist.
Guarantees plus proof beats proof alone. Social proof arriving later never retires
the guarantee grid.

Trust assets are sequenced, not assumed. The strategy works at every stage instead
of only after a portfolio exists.

  client-approved in writing.

  people in grief.

# 9. Page architecture (retained from original draft — this was right)

## Universal service page sequence

1. **Search intent** — literal search phrase in title and H1
   (e.g., "Unattended Death Cleanup in San Jose").
2. **Recognition** — describe the exact situation that caused the search.
3. **Diagnosis** — what the condition may require.
4. **Why the usual solution may not be enough** — reframe without attacking.
5. **Aseptaclean's approach** — the specific process, named stages.
6. **Scope** — what may be addressed; what triggers a stop-and-discuss.
7. **Trust** — guarantees, credentials, document artifacts per the proof ladder.
8. **Fit / not fit** — qualify the visitor; disqualify poor-fit work plainly.
9. **FAQ** — answer the objections preventing contact (cost logic, photos,
   timeline, whether they must enter the property, discretion, what happens if
   more is discovered).
10. **CTA** — Request a Property Assessment. Supporting copy: "Tell us what is
    happening. We will help determine what level of service the property actually
    requires."

## SEO + conversion rule (retained)

Title and H1 use the literal search phrase. Body copy elevates the category and
differentiates. Capture generic demand without becoming a commodity.

## URL namespace at activation

Locked Tier B slate — flat root-level slugs matching the live site's existing URL
pattern, one primary search intent per URL:

| URL | Primary search intent | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/biohazard-cleanup/` | biohazard cleanup san jose | Category page; highest-volume head term; routes to children |
| `/blood-cleanup/` | blood cleanup / blood spill cleanup | Also absorbs "bodily fluid cleanup" — do NOT build a separate bodily-fluid page; same intent, would cannibalize |
| `/unattended-death-cleanup/` | unattended death cleanup | Also absorbs "decomposition cleanup" — one page, decomposition addressed in body copy and FAQ; two pages would split authority on near-identical intent |
| `/crime-scene-cleanup/` | crime scene cleanup | "Crime scene" carries the search volume; "trauma scene" is the regulatory term. URL and H1 use the search phrase; body copy uses "trauma scene" where TSWMP accuracy requires it. Aftermath-only language, always |
| `/human-waste-cleanup/` | human waste / feces cleanup | Distinct intent from animal waste (current scope); cross-link, never merge |
| `/sharps-cleanup/` | needle cleanup / sharps cleanup | H1 can carry "Needle & Sharps Cleanup" to capture both phrasings on one page |

Tier C URLs are reserved but not built until each item's go/no-go clears (§3):
`/encampment-cleanup/`, `/vehicle-biohazard-cleanup/`.

Anti-cannibalization rule: one URL per search intent. Where two search phrases
share an intent (blood/bodily fluid; unattended death/decomposition;
needle/sharps), they share one page — the secondary phrase appears in the H1,
subheads, body, and FAQ, never as its own thin URL. Thin sibling pages split
ranking authority and read as SEO filler, which the quality guardrails already
prohibit for location pages; the same rule applies here.

Current-scope URLs (rodent droppings, animal waste, hoarding, extreme cleaning,
property reset, deep cleaning, move-out, post-construction) are governed by the
live build spec and B-series briefs. They do not appear in this document. Any
draft listing them here is superseded.

No Tier B URL exists in the sitemap, navigation, or internal links before Gate 4.
At activation, keyword targets in this table are re-validated against live SERP
data before briefs are written — search behavior at activation governs, not this
table's assumptions.

## CTA language

Primary: **Request a Property Assessment** (matches sitewide governance — the
original draft's "Request an Assessment" is corrected to the governed phrasing).
Secondary: **Text a Photo of the Property** — with an explicit note on biohazard
pages that photos are optional and no one should re-enter an unsafe scene to take

# 13. Terminology decision: "gross filth" is retired (ACTIVE NOW, not gated)

Owner decision, effective immediately across ALL scopes — current and future:

The term **"gross filth"** is retired from all customer-facing copy, page names,
navigation, headings, and brand vocabulary. It conflicts with the nonjudgmental
tone rules (§4) and shames the exact buyer it addresses — an overwhelmed family
member or adult child who already feels judged.

**Approved replacement:** "extreme cleaning" as the customer-facing service name,
with "severely neglected property" / "severe-condition" as descriptors.

| Context | Old | New |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service name / H1 | Gross Filth Cleanup | Extreme Cleaning |
| Descriptor copy | gross filth conditions | severely neglected property conditions |
| URL (if one exists or is planned) | /gross-filth-cleanup/ | /extreme-cleaning/ |

SEO handling: "gross filth cleanup" is a real search phrase used by some referrers
(landlords, fiduciaries). It may appear ONLY in page metadata and at most once in
body copy in third-person framing ("sometimes called gross filth cleanup") on the
extreme cleaning page — never as a heading, label, or description of a person's
home directed at the reader. This captures the search demand without adopting the
insult as brand vocabulary.

This decision amends current governance and must be logged in
`05-DECISIONS-LOG.md` and swept through all live docs and built pages (see §14).
