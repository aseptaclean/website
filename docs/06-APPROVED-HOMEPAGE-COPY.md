## 8.10 Category contrast section

### Removing the contents is not the same as finishing the property

A hauler may remove what is easy to identify. Dust, cabinet debris, appliance buildup, residue, and unfinished rooms may remain.

A cleaner may handle the surfaces, but only after someone else has emptied and prepared the property.

Aseptaclean defines the clearing and cleaning together. One scope says what remains, what leaves, what gets cleaned, what is excluded, and how completion will be recorded.

That is the difference between an emptied property and a property that is ready for its next handoff.

## 8.11 Five-stage process section

### One company stays accountable from the first decision to the final closeout

You should not have to coordinate a hauler, a cleaner, disposal, access, family approvals, and a final walkthrough just to get one property finished.

The Aseptaclean Five-Stage Handoff Standard keeps the work in one defined path.

#### 1. Scope

We write down what stays, what goes, what gets cleaned, what is excluded, and what the project will require.

#### 2. Protect

Keep areas are identified. Uncertain and important discovered items are separated and reported. We do not decide what mattered to your family.

#### 3. Clear

Approved unwanted contents are consolidated, removed, or coordinated for disposal within the signed scope.

#### 4. Reset

The property receives the detailed cleaning included for its next handoff.

#### 5. Verify

You receive completion photographs, documented exceptions, and a Property Handoff Record showing how the approved scope was closed.

## 8.12 Scope section

### A defined finish, not an open-ended cleanup

Before work begins, the written scope identifies what remains, what leaves, what gets cleaned, and what is outside the job. That is how both sides know what finished means.

#### Work that may be included

- nonhazardous contents clearing
- bagging and consolidation
- light non-structural disassembly
- garage, closet, and storage-area clearing
- kitchen and bathroom deep cleaning
- cabinet and appliance interiors when listed in the scope
- floors, baseboards, doors, closets, and accessible surfaces
- approved disposal or dumpster coordination
- designated keep, remove, and review areas
- starting-condition and completion photographs
- documented exceptions and remote closeout

#### What we do not quietly absorb into the job

We clear and clean properties within a defined operating scope. We are not a licensed contractor, pest-control company, appraisal service, or regulated trauma-scene remediation firm.

Outside the current public scope:

- human blood, bodily fluids, trauma residue, or regulated medical waste
- sewage cleanup
- active mold remediation
- asbestos, lead, unknown chemicals, or hazardous materials
- structural repair, construction, or demolition
- pest extermination or chemical pest treatment
- appraisal, estate-sale, legal, ownership, or habitability determinations
- work requiring a contractor's license

If we observe a condition that requires another qualified specialist, work in the affected area stops. We document what we saw and tell you before the project moves forward.

**You will hear the boundaries before work begins, not halfway through the job.**

The emotional risk in a property clearing project is not only the cost. It is the fear that something important will disappear, the scope will change without warning, or the property will still feel unfinished when the crew leaves.

The Aseptaclean Handoff Assurance addresses those risks directly.

## 8.17 FAQ section

### Can I approve the project remotely?

Yes, when access and decision authority are clear. Scope review, approvals, updates, and closeout can be handled electronically.

### How do you know what should stay?

The written scope identifies keep, remove, and review areas. You decide what remains. Uncertain items are not automatically discarded.

### What happens if you find important documents or valuables?

Discovered keys, documents, photographs, cash, jewelry, and similar items are isolated and reported.

### Can you coordinate a dumpster?

Approved disposal or dumpster coordination may be included when appropriate for the project and within Aseptaclean's lawful and insured operating scope.

### Can you clean after the property has already been emptied?

Yes. Some already-cleared properties may qualify for a defined Handoff Finish scope.

### What can change the price?

Customer-requested changes, concealed conditions, previously undisclosed conditions, disposal changes, access limitations, or specialist-required conditions may require a written change.

### What happens if a hazardous or specialist condition is discovered?

Work in the affected area stops. The condition is documented and you are notified before any next step is taken.

### How quickly can the project begin?

Availability depends on scope, access, labor, disposal requirements, and existing project commitments. Start dates are reserved after scope approval and deposit.

### Can you work with my realtor, property manager, attorney, fiduciary, or family member?

Yes, provided one authorized decision-maker controls approvals and payment.

### Do I have to be present?

Not always. Remote projects can be managed when access, authority, communication, and scope are clear.
